# CTE Expo 2026

**Location:** DNHS Quad

**Extra Credit Theme:** What you saw and what you learned?

## Before the Event

I expected to see a bunch of different booths showing off what students are working on in their CTE classes. I figured it would mostly be presentations and maybe some projects on display but I wasn't sure how hands on it would be.

## Curiosity Before Attending

I was curious what kinds of projects students in other pathways were building and whether any of them connected to what I'm learning in CS.

## What I Saw

I visited a booth where a student was showing off a fully built open frame car, like a dune buggy or kit car, with all the wiring and engine components visible. He also had a detailed poster board titled "How to Jump Start Your Car" that walked through the whole process step by step with diagrams and safety tips.

## Most Interesting Thing I Saw

The car itself was really cool because you could see every single wire and component since there were no body panels covering anything up. The poster was also super detailed, it broke down exactly how to connect jumper cables, which terminals to use, and safety warnings like not jump starting a cracked or leaking battery. The big takeaway on the poster was "Red on Dead" which is an easy way to remember that the red clamp goes on the dead battery's positive terminal first.

## What I Learned

I learned the correct order for connecting jumper cables when jump starting a car. You put the red clamp on the positive terminal of the dead battery first, then the other red clamp on the positive terminal of the booster battery, then the black clamp on the negative terminal of the booster, and the last black clamp goes on an unpainted piece of metal away from the battery, not on the dead battery itself. That last part is important because car batteries produce hydrogen gas which is flammable, so putting the last clip directly on the battery could cause sparks near the gas.

## What Surprised Me

I didn't know that you're supposed to put the last clamp on a piece of metal away from the battery instead of directly on the negative terminal. I also didn't realize you need to drive for at least 20 minutes after jump starting so the alternator can recharge the dead battery. I always thought you could just disconnect and go.

## What Inspired Me

Seeing a student my age who clearly knows his way around a full vehicle build was pretty inspiring. He didn't just build the car, he also made an educational poster to teach other people a practical life skill. That combination of building something and then teaching others about it stood out to me.

## Personal Connection

In CS we focus a lot on building things and solving problems with software, but seeing someone do the same thing with hardware and mechanical systems reminded me that the problem solving mindset is the same across different fields. The wiring in that car honestly looked like a physical version of a circuit diagram or system architecture.

## Interaction

**Person or group I learned from:** A student presenting at the auto tech booth who built the car and made the poster.

He showed the exposed engine and wiring of the car and had the poster explaining the full process of how to jump start a vehicle. The poster covered everything from positioning the cars to connecting cables in the right order, safety precautions like not using a cracked battery, and making sure both cars are off and in park before you start.

## Depth of Experience

It made me appreciate how much engineering and problem solving goes into fields outside of computer science. Seeing all that wiring and the engine setup made me think about how similar the logical thinking is between building a car system and building a software system. Both require you to understand how different parts connect and what happens when something goes wrong.

## Connection to CS or Future Goals

It connects to CS in the sense that debugging and troubleshooting are universal skills. The way you trace wires in a car to find a problem is a lot like tracing through code to find a bug. It also made me more interested in hardware and embedded systems, which is where CS and physical engineering overlap.

## Final Reflection

It was cool to see what students in other CTE pathways are doing and realize that we're all learning the same core skills just in different ways. The expo made the school feel more connected and showed me that there's a lot of creativity and real world problem solving happening outside of just my classes.

## Biggest Takeaway

> Red on dead, and the problem solving skills you build in any technical field carry over to everything else you do.
