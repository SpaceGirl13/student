# CTE Expo Early Speaker

**Date:** May 21

**Location:** Del Norte High School A101

**Extra Credit Theme:** What inspired you?

## Proof of Experience

![CTE]({{site.baseurl}}/images/cte-expo-image.jpeg)


## Before the Event

I expected a pretty standard career talk. I didn't think it would get so detailed and practical about what it actually takes to start a company from scratch, especially one focused on something like voting.

## Curiosity Before Attending

I wanted to know how a football player ended up in tech and what skills from sports actually helped him in business.

## What I Saw

Elijah Johnson's panel on his entrepreneurship journey and his company SafeBallot, which is a blockchain based digital voting system.

## Most Interesting Thing I Saw

The way Elijah used community stuff like adopting a highway, sponsoring a basketball team, and giving out youth leadership grants to build his brand. None of that has anything to do with voting directly, but it helped people trust him and got his name out there. He talked about how you have to meet people where they're at before you can bring them in the door.

## What I Learned

When Elijah couldn't afford to hire a cybersecurity company, he formed a partnership instead. He got on their board and helped them out, and in return they helped secure SafeBallot. It showed me that you can get really far just by offering value and building relationships even when you don't have money.

## What Surprised Me

I was surprised that SafeBallot is being used right now as an educational tool to teach 4th and 5th graders how to campaign and vote. I assumed it was aimed at real government elections, but starting with kids in schools is actually a really smart way to build the product up and prove it works.

## What Inspired Me

Elijah said to close your eyes and picture the best possible outcome for yourself or your business, and then scale it down to what you can actually do today. That really stuck with me because it makes big goals feel way less overwhelming.

## Personal Connection

I sometimes feel like I need to master everything before I can start building something real. Hearing that Elijah launched businesses with whatever he had at the time and let each one teach him something for the next one made me want to just start working on stuff now instead of waiting until I feel ready.

## Interaction

**Person or group I learned from:** Elijah Johnson, the founder of SafeBallot. He's a former football player and serial entrepreneur who started with things like cleaning shoes and door to door sales before getting into tech.

He explained how he saw a problem with the voting system and used blockchain to build a secure digital platform where votes can't be changed or hacked. He also talked a lot about how to handle investors, when to say no to money, and why community outreach matters just as much as the product itself.

## Depth of Experience

It changed my mindset from "I need to have everything figured out before I start" to "just start with what you have and figure it out along the way." It also showed me that being good at the technical side isn't enough. You also need to be able to communicate, build relationships, and connect with people on a real level.

## Connection to CS or Future Goals

The blockchain and cybersecurity parts of SafeBallot tie right into CS topics like data security and making sure information can't be tampered with. Beyond that, his focus on pitching ideas and working with partners reminded me that CS careers are about way more than just coding. You have to be able to explain what you're building and work with other people to make it happen.

## Final Reflection

It was meaningful because Elijah didn't just give generic advice. He shared real stories about failing and trying again across multiple businesses. It made the whole idea of starting something feel doable, not just something for people with tons of money or connections already.

## Biggest Takeaway

> You don't need everything figured out to start, just work with what you have, build real connections, and let every experience set you up for what comes next.
